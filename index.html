Welcome

Welcome to my academic website.

I am Devasmito Das, a researcher working at the intersection of control theory, nonlinear dynamics, fractional-order systems, chaos, predictive control and robotics.

My research focuses on understanding complex nonlinear dynamical systems and developing advanced control strategies for systems exhibiting memory, uncertainty, chaotic behaviour, strong nonlinearities and network interactions.

I am currently a Maître de conférences at the Université de Montpellier, with my research activities based at LIRMM. My current research focuses primarily on the modelling, analysis, and advanced control of soft and continuum robotic systems, as well as cable-driven parallel robots (CDPRs), with particular interest in nonlinear and predictive control, robustness, state estimation, constraint handling, and real-time implementation.

About Me

I received my Ph.D. through a joint doctoral programme between École Centrale de Nantes, France, and the Indian Institute of Technology Madras, India.

My doctoral research focused on coupled networks of fractional nonlinear oscillators, with particular emphasis on fractional dynamics, synchronization, chaos, nonlinear control, predictive control and learning-based ocntrol.

My broader research objective is to develop mathematically sound and computationally efficient control methodologies for nonlinear dynamical systems with memory, uncertainty, constraints and complex interactions.

My current research interests increasingly extend toward robotic systems, including soft and continuum robots, cable-driven robots, nonlinear predictive control, adaptive control and physics-informed intelligent control.

Research

Nonlinear and Fractional-Order Dynamics

Fractional-order systems provide mathematical models with intrinsic memory and hereditary effects. My work investigates their nonlinear behaviour, chaos, synchronization, stability and control.

Chaos Control

I study feedback strategies for suppressing, stabilizing and exploiting chaotic behaviour in nonlinear systems, including Rössler, Duffing, Lorenz-type and networked oscillators.

Model Predictive Control

My research includes linear and nonlinear model predictive control for constrained dynamical systems, including fractional-order systems, chaotic dynamics and robotic applications.

Networked Dynamical Systems

I investigate synchronization, collective dynamics and control of interconnected nonlinear oscillators and large-scale dynamical networks.

Robotics

Current directions include nonlinear and predictive control of soft robots, continuum robots and cable-driven robotic systems, with particular interest in real-time control, robustness, state estimation and safety constraints.

Learning-Based Control

I am also interested in combining classical control theory with machine learning, reinforcement learning and physics-informed methods while maintaining stability, interpretability and robustness.

Fractional-Order Chaotic Oscillators

Modelling, analysis, identification, and control of nonlinear oscillators governed by fractional-order dynamics, with particular emphasis on memory effects, chaos, stability, synchronization, and numerical characterization.

Chaos Suppression using Predictive Control

Development of model predictive and nonlinear control frameworks for stabilization, chaos suppression, and synchronization of strongly nonlinear dynamical systems under constraints and uncertainty.

Coupled Oscillator Networks

Investigation of synchronization, collective behaviour, and control in heterogeneous networks of nonlinear and fractional-order oscillators, including memory-dependent and large-scale dynamical systems.

Soft and Continuum Robotics

Nonlinear modelling, state estimation, and advanced control of compliant robotic systems, with current emphasis on soft and continuum robots, viscoelastic dynamics, predictive control, robustness, and real-time implementation.

Cable-Driven Parallel Robots

Development of robust, adaptive, and nonlinear model predictive control strategies for cable-driven parallel robots, incorporating unilateral tension constraints, workspace feasibility, uncertain payloads, and safety-related operating limits.

Large-Scale Chaotic Systems

Investigation of actuation accessibility, stabilization limits, and control performance in high-dimensional nonlinear systems, including multiscale Lorenz-96 dynamics and other complex networked models.
